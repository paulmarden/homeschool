The patriarchal world of William Shakespeare's 'A Midsummer Night's Dream'

View on Oak Academy

I can explore how the patriarchy is presented in William Shakespeare’s ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’, focusing on the openings of Act 1 and Act 2.

Key learning points
  1. Athens is a patriarchal society.
  2. Egeus has the right to kill his daughter.
  3. Act 1 and Act 2 both begin with a conflict.
  4. It could be argued that Titania is presented as Oberon's equal.
  5. Oberon has to use a magical love potion on Titania in order to get what he wants.

3In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', who does Titania fall in love with when under the influence of the magical love potion?
Hint
Consider that Titania doesn't interact with the Athenian nobles.
Show answer
AnswerBottom
WhyPuck puts the magical potion on Titania as she is sleeping. When she wakes up, she falls in love with one of the Athenian actors, Bottom. Puck makes this relationship even more ridiculous by transforming Bottom's head into that of a donkey.
4In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', there is lots of conflict throughout the play. Match the pairs of characters with their conflict.
Egeus and Hermia
Titania and Oberon
Demetrius and Helena
Helena and Hermia
Hint
Start with Act 1. Consider that the play begins in Athens, not the magical forest. Why is there conflict in Athens?
Show answer
AnswerEgeus and Hermia → argue about who Hermia should marry; Titania and Oberon → argue about who gets to be in charge of a little boy; Demetrius and Helena → argue at the beginning of the play because one doesn't love the other; Helena and Hermia → argue over which man loves them and why
WhyThere are many problems between fathers and daughters, and different partners. The magical love potion increases the conflict as it causes chaos and confusion. But at the end of the play, all of the conflicts are resolved.

1In Act 1 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', Hermia is told she will be punished if she doesn't obey her father. What punishments could she face?

Select all that apply.

Hint
Consider that Hermia wants to marry Lysander.
Show answer
Answerhaving to marry Demetrius; having to become a nun; being killed
WhyHermia's punishments are extreme: a forced marriage, imprisonment in a nunnery or death. Although the play is a comedy, it also contains some darker elements and ideas, which are clearly displayed in Act 1.
2In what ways are the openings of Acts 1 and 2 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream' similar?
Hint
Consider that two of the play's Acts are set in Athens. Which are they?
Show answer
Answerboth start with conflict
WhyIn Act 1, we see a father-daughter conflict presented. In Act 2, we see a conflict between two partners. In both cases, men make demands and threats of the women they are supposed to love.
3In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', how does Titania respond when Oberon asks her to give him the little boy she looks after?
Hint
Consider that Puck is Oberon's servant, not Titania's.
Show answer
AnswerShe says yes, but only after he makes her fall in love with Bottom.
WhyWhen Oberon demands, "Give me that boy", Titania replies, "Not for thy fairy kingdom". It is only through trickery and magic that Oberon can get what he wants.
4In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', Athens is presented as a society, a society in which men hold the power and the laws and structure of the society prevent women from having power.
Hint

Show answer
Answerpatriarchal
WhyAthens is presented as a patriarchal society. We see this through the presentation of Egeus who is able to control and punish his daughter as he sees fit because he is her father.

WhyThis simile conveys the patriarchal society of Athens, one in which men are considered superior to women, and the expectation is that women obey men.
