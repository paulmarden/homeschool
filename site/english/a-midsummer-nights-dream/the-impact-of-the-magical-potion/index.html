The impact of the magical potion in 'A Midsummer Night's Dream'

View on Oak Academy

I can evaluate how the magical potion is used by Oberon and Puck, and the impact it has on different characters in ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’.

Key learning points
  1. The magical potion is used twice in Act 2, Scene 2.
  2. Oberon has malicious intentions; Puck is trying to carry out Oberon’s orders.
  3. Lysander falls in love with Helena, who believes Lysander is mocking her.
  4. Titania falls in love with Bottom, and makes him stay with her in the forest.
  5. When writing about ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’, connect different moments of the play together.

Exit quiz

6 questions
1In Act 2, Scene 2 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', the magical potion is used twice. How is it used?

Select all that apply.

Hint
Consider that the magical potion is mistakenly applied to someone in this scene.
Show answer
AnswerOberon uses it to make Titania fall in love with someone humiliating.; Puck uses it on Lysander, thinking he is Demetrius.
WhyAlthough the same potion is used twice, there are very different intentions behind its use. Oberon has malicious intentions when he uses it on Titania, but Puck doesn't mean to create chaos and confusion between the Athenian noble people.
2In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', how does Helena react when Lysander says he is in love with her?
Hint
Consider that the Athenian women are presented as very constant in their love for Lysander and Demetrius. They don't alter who they love at any point in the play.
Show answer
AnswerShe feels he is mocking her.
WhyHelena truly believes Lysander is teasing her by pretending to be in love with her, asking what she has done to "deserve this scorn".
3In Act 2, Scene 2 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', how does Hermia feel when she wakes up and finds herself alone in the forest without Lysander?

Select all that apply.

Hint
Consider that the Athenian noble people do not know that they are in a supernatural forest filled with fairies.
Show answer
Answerdetermined to find him; very frightened
WhyOn the one hand, Hermia is presented as very scared - she almost faints with fear. On the other hand, she quickly forms a plan and seems willing to risk death to find Lysander. Her bravery here perhaps reminds us of her bravery in Act 1.
4In Act 3 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', Titania wakes up after being anointed with the magical potion in her sleep. She sees Bottom, and says, "What wakes me from my flowery bed?".
Hint
Consider this word describes a supernatural being, often associated with extreme goodness and virtue.
Show answer
Answerangel
WhyThis question is designed to contrast with the character of Bottom himself. He is not an angel, merely a slightly ridiculous man, made yet more ridiculous because his head has been transformed into that of a donkey.
5In Act 2 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', Oberon anoints Titania's eyes with the magical potion. As he does, he says "Wake when some thing is near".
Hint
Consider this is a synonym for horrible.
Show answer
Answervile
WhyThe speech when Oberon anoints Titania's eyes makes it clear his malicious intentions. He wants her new love to distract her so he can take the boy from her but he also wants the creature she falls in love with to be something horrible.
6In Act 2 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', Oberon anoints Titania's eyes with the magical potion. He wants her to "love and " for the creature she falls in love with.
Hint
Consider this phrase uses alliteration, and the missing word is a synonym for weaken.
Show answer
Answerlanguish
WhyOberon needs Titania to "languish" (become weaker) so that he can take the boy he wants from her. In this way, you could argue that Shakespeare is actually showing how powerful Titania is, as Oberon needs to use magic to overpower her.
