Act 3 of ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’: disorder in the forest

View on Oak Academy

I can explore Act 3 of ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’, focusing on the disordered relationships between the Athenian nobles and Titania and Bottom.

Key learning points
  1. Oberon is pleased that Titania has fallen in love with Bottom.
  2. Oberon is angry that Puck put the magical potion on the wrong Athenian man.
  3. The relationship between Titania and Bottom is an example of disorder.
  4. For Elizabethans, Titania and Bottom’s relationship would be seen as especially funny because it disrupts the hierarchy.
  5. Elizabethans believed that everyone had their place in a hierarchical system called The Great Chain of Being.

Hint

AnswerShe is a monarch.; She is supernatural.; She is tricked by Oberon.
WhyShakespeare establishes Titania's power in her opening scene, where she is said to enter with a "train" of servants, and refuses to give in to Oberon's demands. Later, she is tricked by Oberon - both a source of comedy, but also humiliation.
2In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', what do we learn about the character of Bottom?

Select all that apply.

Hint
Consider that three characters have the magical love potion used on them. Who are they?
Show answer
AnswerHe is a skilled manual labourer from Athens.; He wants to perform a play for Duke Theseus.
WhyBottom is meant to be a figure of fun in the play. He is transformed by Puck, and then loved by Titania while looking like a donkey. At the end of the play, he gets to perform for the noble people, oblivious to what has happened to him in the forest.
