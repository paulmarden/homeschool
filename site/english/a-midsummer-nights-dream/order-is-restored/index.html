Act 3, Scene 2 of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream': order is restored in the forest

View on Oak Academy

I can explain how the disorder in the forest is resolved, and consider how happy the ending of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream' is.

Key learning points
  1. In Act 3, Scene 1, we see Puck’s ability to create disorder amongst the Rude Mechanicals.
  2. In Act 3, Scene 2, we see Puck use the same abilities to restore order amongst the Athenian nobles.
  3. Puck applies the antidote to Lysander, but not Demetrius.
  4. Oberon asks for, and gets, the little boy he wants from Titania.
  5. The resolution to the play is happy, but audiences might question the treatment of Titania and Demetrius.

1In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', why is Helena so unhappy at the beginning of the play?
Hint
Consider that Helena's father is not a character in the play.
Show answer
AnswerDemetrius doesn't love her.
WhyIn Act 1, Demetrius loves Hermia. After a night in the forest, and the magical potion, he falls in love with Helena allowing for the play to end with two marriages: Lysander and Hermia's, and Demetrius's and Helena's.
2In 'A Midsummer Night's Dream', why are Oberon and Titania in conflict at the beginning of Act 2?
Hint
Consider that Oberon and Puck are the only characters who know about, and use, the magical potion.
Show answer
AnswerTitania refuses to give Oberon a little boy he wants.
WhyAt the beginning of Act 2, we see Titania as a powerful monarch who refuses to give into Oberon's demands. However, by the end of the play, Oberon has the little boy he wants, having used trickery to get it.

WhyAfter getting the little boy, Oberon removes the magical potion. Although this resolves the conflict between the two monarchs, audiences may question just how fair or happy this resolution is.
