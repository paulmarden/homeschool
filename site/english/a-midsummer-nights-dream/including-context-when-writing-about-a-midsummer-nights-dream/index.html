Including context when writing about Puck in 'A Midsummer Night's Dream'

View on Oak Academy

I can include relevant contextual ideas in my writing when exploring the character of Puck in ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’.

Content guidance
Depiction or discussion of discriminatory behaviourDiscriminatory behaviour or images, whether on the grounds of race, gender, disability, religion or belief or sexual orientation, or otherwise. · Language and discrimination
Key learning points
  1. Shakespeare was writing for an Elizabethan audience.
  2. The ideas, characters and plot points in the play are designed to engage with Elizabethan society, attitudes and values.
  3. Information about Elizabethan society, attitudes and values is called context.
  4. When writing about the play, you should include context to develop your ideas.
Keywords
patriarchala society in which men hold the power; the laws and structure of that society prevent women having power.
hierarchya system whereby people or things are ranked, with some being at the top, and others lower down.
Elizabethana person alive when Elizabeth I was on the throne (1558-1603); the first audiences of ‘A Midsummer Night’s Dream’ were Elizabethan.
folklorestories and traditions that are passed down from generation to generation; these are not written down, but told verbally.
Shakespearean comedya play designed to make you laugh, often involving misunderstandings and disorder, ending in marriage.
Equipment
You need access to a copy of Shakespeare's 'A Midsummer Night's Dream'.
