Maya Angelou's poetic voice

View on Oak Academy

I can comment on Maya Angelou's poetic voice, referencing a number of her poems to support my ideas.

Key learning points
  1. We can explore Angelou's poems as a collection, as well as individually.
  2. Exploring Angelou's poems as a collection allows us to understand her as a poet.
  3. We might make comments on similarities between speakers, ideas and methods across poems.
  4. We can use these comments to consider Angelou's poetic voice as a whole, and suggest the dominant tone across her work.
Keywords

You need access to 'Phenomenal Woman', 'Woman Work', 'Still I Rise', 'On Aging', 'Caged Bird', 'Equality', 'Life Doesn't Frighten Me, and 'On the Pulse of the Morning' (Penguin Random House).

Hint
Consider that in Angelou's 'Life Doesn't Frighten Me', the poem's title is used as the refrain.
Show answer
Answercommands → orders; refrain → repeated line or group of lines in a poem; tone → the attitude of emotion of your voice - written or verbal
WhyWhen writing a poem, you will consider how you want to convey your ideas. Commands and refrains are just two examples of methods you can use (if you wish!). You will also consider the overall tone you want to convey.

Answer'Woman Work' → a lonely Black female speaker lists the chores she must complete; 'Phenomenal Woman' → an assertive speaker explains why she is so extraordinary; 'Still I Rise' → a defiant speaker explains why she can't be oppressed; 'Caged Bird' → explores the Jim Crow laws through extended metaphor; 'Equality' → a commanding speaker demands equal treatment
WhyAngelou was a prolific poet and wrote about a range of ideas with a range of speakers. However, there are ideas and voices that she kept coming back to. These short summaries show a few of the ideas she liked returning to.
