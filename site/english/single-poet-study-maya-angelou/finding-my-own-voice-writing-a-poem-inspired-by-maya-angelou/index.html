Finding my own voice: writing a poem inspired by Maya Angelou

View on Oak Academy

I can write a poem inspired by the ideas and methods in Angelou's poetry.

Key learning points
  1. You can use a poet's work to inspire your own creativity.
  2. You can plan each stanza of a poem by considering its focus, details and methods
  3. Reading your poem aloud can help you identify areas you would like to improve.

Equipment
You need access to 'Phenomenal Woman', 'Woman Work', 'Still I Rise', 'On Aging', 'Caged Bird', 'Equality', 'Life Doesn't Frighten Me', and 'On the Pulse of the Morning' (Penguin Random House).

refrain
rhyme
alliteration
sibilance
Hint
Consider that Angelou's poem 'Woman Work' uses alliteration in the title.
Show answer
Answerrefrain → a repeated line or lines throughout a poem; rhyme → using words that sound like each other in the middle or end of lines; alliteration → using the same letter to start two or more words; sibilance → the repetition of the 's' sound
WhyWhen writing creatively, you will consider the methods you want to use to convey your ideas. These are just four examples. You could try using each in your next piece of creative writing to explore their effects and power!

Show answer

Why'On Aging', 'Phenomenal Woman' and 'Equality' are all titles which state, literally, what the poem will be about. 'Caged Bird' is a metaphor, as is the title 'Still I Rise'. The speaker is not literally "rising". The bird in the cage is a metaphor.
