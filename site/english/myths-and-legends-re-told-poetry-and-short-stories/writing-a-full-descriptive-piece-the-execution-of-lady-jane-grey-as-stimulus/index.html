Writing a full descriptive piece: ‘The Execution of Lady Jane Grey' as stimulus

View on Oak Academy

I can write a well-structured and developed piece of descriptive writing inspired by the painting, ‘The Execution of Lady Jane Grey’.

Content guidance
Depiction or discussion of sensitive contentSubjects which particular individuals may be sensitive to, eg, adoption, migration, bereavement or death, divorce, poverty, deprivation. · Upsetting, disturbing and sensitive
Depiction or discussion of violence or sufferingDepiction or discussion of violence or fighting, including threats of violence or the aftermath of violence. · Upsetting, disturbing and sensitive
Key learning points
  1. In descriptive writing, sometimes developing the description of small details can be very engaging.
  2. To structure your piece, you could link the concluding sentence of paragraphs to the next topic sentence.
  3. To structure your piece, your first and final paragraph could repeat selected ideas or images.

topic sentence
supporting details
concluding sentence

WhyThe key to successful writing is detailed and careful planning. A single paragraph outline can support this planning by making you think hard about the focus of each paragraph, and the detail to help develop it.

4A omniscient narrator is when the narrator isn't a character in the story and presents the feelings and experiences of multiple characters using pronouns like 'she', 'he', 'they', 'it'.
Hint
Other types of narrator include first person and second person.
Show answer
Answerthird person / 3rd person
WhyYou might choose a third person narrative voice so you can offer a range of perspectives in your piece. If you choose first person, you can develop a character, but don't have access to other characters' thoughts, feelings, experiences and contexts.

6Which sentences, all inspired by 'The Execution of Lady Jane Grey' ('Myths, legends and stories that inspire'), use the word suppress (or a form of it) correctly?

AnswerShe wanted to cry out, but she suppressed her sobs.; There were many emotions in the cell, but each chose to suppress them.
WhySuppress is a verb, not an adjective. You can't look around suppressively because it means to bottle up an emotion, or keep it hidden.
