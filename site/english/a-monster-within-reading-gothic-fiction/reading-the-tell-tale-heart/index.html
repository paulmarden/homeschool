Reading 'The Tell-Tale Heart'

View on Oak Academy

I can explain the overarching plot of the 'Tell Tale Heart'.

Depiction or discussion of violence or sufferingDepiction or discussion of violence or fighting, including threats of violence or the aftermath of violence. · Upsetting, disturbing and sensitive
Key learning points
  1. ’The Tell-Tale Heart’ is the story about the murder of an old man.
  2. The narrator did not murder for money but for fear of the man's blue eyes.
  3. The narrator kills the man as he worries a neighbour may hear the loud beating of his heart.
  4. The narrator dismembers the body and hides it under the floorboards.
  5. The narrator continues to hear the beating heart of the dead man, which leads him to confess.

6Starting with the first, put the events of 'The Tell-Tale Heart' in chronological order.
On the 8th night, the narrator accidentally wakes the old man up.
The narrator chuckles at the thought of the old man's fear.
The narrator hears the old man's heart beating and hysterically confesses.
The narrator sneaks into the old man's room seven nights in a row.
The narrator tells us about his hatred of the old man's 'evil eye'.
The narrator thinks he hears the old man's heart beating, so he kills him.
The police visit, but they are unaware that the body is under the floorboards.
Hint
Start by identifying the reason as to why the narrator commits murder. Why does he want to kill the old man?
Show answer
AnswerThe narrator tells us about his hatred of the old man's 'evil eye'. → The narrator sneaks into the old man's room seven nights in a row. → On the 8th night, the narrator accidentally wakes the old man up. → The narrator chuckles at the thought of the old man's fear. → The narrator thinks he hears the old man's heart beating, so he kills him. → The police visit, but they are unaware that the body is under the floorboards. → The narrator hears the old man's heart beating and hysterically confesses.
WhyThe narrator's delusions escalate as the story progresses, thereby allowing Poe to build tension in the lead up to the narrator's confession.
