Problem solving with perimeter, area and volume

View on Oak Academy

I can use my knowledge of perimeter, area and volume to solve problems.

Key learning points
  1. The volume and surface area of shapes made from prisms and cylinders can be found.
  2. The volume and surface area of shapes made from parts of prisms and cylinders can be found.
  3. Unknown lengths can be found when surface area or volume are known.
  4. Volume and surface area can be used in context to solve problems.
Keywords
Prism
polyhedron with a base that is a polygon and identical parallel opposite face whose corresponding edges are joined by parallelograms.
Cylinder
3D shape with a circle base and an identical parallel opposite face. Cross sections made parallel to the base will be congruent to the base.
Surface area
the total area of all the surfaces of a closed 3D shape. The surfaces include all faces and any curved surfaces.
Volume
the amount of space occupied by a closed 3D shape.

6A cylinder has a volume of $$300\pi\text{ cm}^3$$ and a length of $$3\text{ cm}$$. The surface area of the cylinder is $$\pi\text{ cm}^2$$.
Hint
Start by finding the area of the circular cross-section.
Show answer
Answer260 / two hundred and sixty
WhyArea of circular end face = $$300 \pi \div 3 = 100 \pi $$. Since area = $$\pi r^2$$ then $$r^2 = 100$$ and $$r=10$$. The area of the curved surface is $$2 \pi \times 10 \times 3 = 60 \pi$$. So the total surface area is $$100 \pi+100 \pi +60 \pi $$.
