Finding the original amount after a decrease

View on Oak Academy

I can calculate the original value, given the final value after a stated percentage decrease.

Key learning points
  1. A bar model can be used to find the original amount after a percentage decrease.
  2. A double number line can be used to find the original amount after a percentage decrease.
  3. A ratio table can be used to find the original amount after a percentage decrease.
  4. In all of these representations a single multiplier can be used to find the original amount after a percentage decrease.
Keywords
Reciprocal
The reciprocal is the multiplicative inverse of any non-zero number. Any non-zero number multiplied by its reciprocal is equal to 1.

Answer300 × 0.2
WhyThe decimal equivalent of 20% is 0.2, so 0.2 is the multiplier. The correct calculation is 300 × 0.2
2A number is increased by 25%. 1 part is equal to 25%. What will be the total number of parts representing the new number?

Answer5
Why100% ÷ 4 = 25% so the original is 4 parts. Increasing by 25% means an extra 1 part, giving 5 parts in total.

4Jun thinks of a number and increases it by 20%. Jun's new number is 720. What was his original number?
Hint
Divide by the multiplier for an increase of 20%.
Show answer
Answer600
WhyAn increase of 20% is the same as 100% + 20% = 120% which is a multiplier of 1.2. So, 720 ÷ 1.2 = 600

3Match each percentage question to the correct multiplier.
Increase by 2%
Increase by 30%
Decrease by 20%
Decrease by 3%
Hint
Start with 100% and increase or decrease by the percentage and convert this to a multiplier.
Show answer
AnswerIncrease by 2% → 1.02; Increase by 30% → 1.3; Decrease by 20% → 0.8; Decrease by 3% → 0.97
Why100% + 2% = 102% which is 1.02 as a multiplier. 100% + 30% = 130% which is 1.3 as a multiplier. 100% − 20% = 80% which is 0.8 as a multiplier. 100% − 3% = 97% which is 0.97 as a multiplier.
