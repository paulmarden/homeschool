Lesson 9 of 12

Finding the nth term

View on Oak Academy

I can find the n^th term rule by investigating the common difference.

Key learning points
  1. Finding the common difference can help when finding the n^th term rule.
  2. Comparing the sequence to an appropriate multiplication table can help identify the translation that has been made.
  3. The n^th term can be found for all arithmetic sequences.
  4. The n^th term rule can be used to identify the term number of a given number in a sequence.
Keywords
n^th term
The nth term of a sequence is the position of a term in a sequence where n stands for the term number.
Common misconceptions
That the sequence 6,11,16,21, ... is 5n+6 because it goes up by 5 and starts at 6.
Compare 6,11,16,21, ... to 5,10,15,20, ... "What is the shift? The translation? If that is 5n then this is 5n with how much more?"
Teaching tips
Get students to explain back to you why 6,11,16,21, ... is 5n+1 and NOT 5n+6. If they can counter-argue the misconception then they truly understand what is going on here.

AnswerAdd 3

3What is the position-to-term rule for this pattern sequence? [image]
Hint
A position-to-term rule describes how to generate the term from the term number.
Show answer
AnswerMultiply the term number by 3 and add 1
WhyThe first pattern is $$(1) \times 3 + 1 = 4$$ squares. The second is $$(2) \times 3 + 1 = 7$$ squares and so on.

Hint
An arithmetic (linear) sequence is a sequence where the difference between successive terms is a constant.
Show answer
Answer1, 4, 7, 10, ...; 10, 7, 4, 1, ...; 0.4, 0.7, 1, 1.3, ...
Why1, 4, 7, 10, ... has a constant difference +3. For 10, 7, 4, 1, ... it's -3, and for 0.4, 0.7, 1, 1.3, ... it's +0.3, whereas 1, 2, 4, 8, ... and 1, 4, 8, 13, ... do not have a constant difference.
