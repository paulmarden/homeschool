Lesson 18 of 19

Statistical problems - drawing conclusions

View on Oak Academy

I can effectively summarise and communicate conclusions to a statistical problem.

Key learning points
  1. Conclusions can be drawn from statistical summaries and graphical representations.
  2. Conclusions are understood to be limited by any biases.
  3. Conclusions are understood to only be valid for this sample of data.
Keywords
population
The population is the entire set of people, creatures, plants or items that make up the whole group which is being studied
sample
A sample is a subset of the population.
bias
Bias can be present in any sample collected from a population. e.g. only asking year 7 pupils about a whole school issue.
statistical summary
A statistical summary sums up the features of a data set

Hint

AnswerAs the temperature increases, the number of ice cream sales also increases.
WhyA positive correlation suggests that as one variable increases, the other variable increases too. The temperature rise causes people to eat more ice cream. People eating more ice cream doesn't cause the temperature to rise!

WhyThe 4 numbers sum to 36, so the mean is 9.
