Constructing pie charts

View on Oak Academy

I can construct pie charts from data.

Key learning points
  1. Data presented as a list can be represented as a pie chart.
  2. Data presented in a table can be represented as a pie chart.
  3. A larger data set requires considering the proportion of the total.
Keywords
Sector
A sector is the region formed between two radii and their connecting arc.

1Match the descriptions for amounts of rotation with the number of degrees each is equivalent to.
an eighth of a turn
a quarter turn
a half turn
a three-quarter turn
a full turn
Hint
A quarter turn is 90°, and an eighth of a turn is half this amount.
Show answer
Answeran eighth of a turn → 45°; a quarter turn → 90°; a half turn → 180°; a three-quarter turn → 270°; a full turn → 360°
WhyA quarter, half, three-quarter, and full turn are all multiples of 90°, whilst an eighth of a turn is half of a quarter turn. A half of 90° is 45°.

Hint
To find a fraction of an amount, you can find $${1}\over{5}$$ of 40 first by dividing by 5.
Show answer
Answer16 / sixteen
Why$$${1}\over{5}$$ of 40 = 8, therefore $${2}\over{5}$$ of 40 = 16.

How many 8s are there in 24? Check you included a unit in your answer.

4If 5 footballs cost £30, then how much would 8 footballs cost?
Hint
First find the multiplier that gets you from 5 footballs to £30. This can be done by calculating £30 $$\div$$ 5.
Show answer
Answer£48
WhyThe multiplier is 6, as 5 footballs multiplied by 6 = £30. This same multiplier is applied to 8 footballs. 8 $$\times$$ 6 = £48.
5If 80 table tennis balls cost £16, then how many table tennis balls would cost £3?
Hint
First find the multiplier that gets you from £16 to 80 table tennis balls. This can be done by calculating 80 $$\div$$ 16.
Show answer
Answer15
WhyThe multiplier is 5, as £16 multiplied by 5 = 80 table tennis balls. This same multiplier is applied to £3. 3 $$\times$$ 5 = 15 tennis balls.

5Starting with the first step, put these steps for constructing a sector of a pie chart in order.
Draw a straight line from centre of the circle through the × you plot.
Draw a straight line from the centre of the circle to its circumference.
Line the 0° on the protractor with the most recent straight line drawn.
Line the bottom centre of the protractor with the centre of the circle.
Plot an × at this point on the protractor.
Remove your protractor.
Starting from 0°, find the angle you want to draw on the protractor.
Hint
You need to use your ruler, then correctly align the protractor before you focus on finding any angles on the protractor.
Show answer
AnswerDraw a straight line from the centre of the circle to its circumference. → Line the bottom centre of the protractor with the centre of the circle. → Line the 0° on the protractor with the most recent straight line drawn. → Starting from 0°, find the angle you want to draw on the protractor. → Plot an × at this point on the protractor. → Remove your protractor. → Draw a straight line from centre of the circle through the × you plot.
WhyIt is important to place your protractor carefully, aligning it with the centre of the circle and the most recent line you've drawn as a priority. If this is not done, then any angle found will not be drawn in the correct location.
